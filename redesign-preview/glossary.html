Fitts's Law

The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to it and inversely a function of its size — bigger, closer targets are faster and more accurate to hit.

Example

macOS puts the menu bar at the screen's top edge — the edge makes the target effectively infinite in height.

Why it matters: mis-sized tap targets measurably increase error rate and time-on-task.

Hick's Law

Decision time increases logarithmically with the number and complexity of choices — more visible options always cost more time.

Example

6 curated meal categories up front, with everything else behind "See all," beats a flat list of 80 items.

Jakob's Law

Users spend most of their time on other products, so they prefer your interface to work the way the ones they already know work.

Example

Cart icon top-right on an e-commerce site — muscle memory from Amazon makes it the fastest place to find it.

Miller's Law (7±2)

Unaided short-term memory holds only a few chunks at once — chunking related items extends what people can actually track.

Example

Splitting a 16-field signup form into four ~4-field steps instead of one long scrolling form.
